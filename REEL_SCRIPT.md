- **Contrast moment (0:33).** The two bars are the same idea as the "Same playbook, Opposite ending" fork: two things side by side, one in mint green, one grey.
- **Payoff (0:42).** The checklist ticks as he says each word and turns red on "very difficult", so the viewer sees the point before he finishes the sentence.
- **Captions.** One bold word at a time, white with a soft shadow, under his chin, with the emphasis words in mint green. On every shot they stay below his chin and never cover his face.
- **Look.** Dark teal-to-black gradient, one mint-green accent, thin glowing outlines, small pill labels. Dev is full-frame in the lower half, with no box.

### Part 1: Cold open (0:20)

**On camera:** In the past year, we've done more deal sourcing than some people do in 10-year careers in private equity. And this was extremely surprising for me to learn, and at the same time, quite encouraging. Capital is abundant. Talent can be found. Deal flow is very difficult.

### Part 2: Intro and why London (1:15)

**On camera:** What's up guys? I want to do a bit of a debrief from the last couple of days of events and meetings. They've been honestly very, very busy. I've been sleeping five, six hours a day, waking up a lot earlier than I'm used to. It's 9:00 AM here, it's 1:00 PM in India, so the jet lag is helping quite a bit.

*Source: 0618, 00:11-00:47.*

**On camera:** So the first question you're probably asking is, why did I fly to London? I went for the Rollup Europe event, and I went with two agendas.

**On camera:** The first was my own search. I'm currently exploring buying a business. My main thesis is in India, because that's where I'm from and where I see a lot of opportunity. But the second thesis, which is getting more serious now, is buying a business in Italy. There are a lot of succession issues with family businesses there, and the market is very fragmented. There are plenty of buyers above 50 or 100 million, but very few looking at 2 to 10 million in enterprise value. That's the range I want to explore, and not for just one acquisition, but multiple. So I wanted to battle-test the thesis, ask investors and Italian entrepreneurs whether it has legs, and build a network I can lean on as I search.

*Source: 0619, 00:37-02:07.*

**On camera:** The second agenda was to meet potential clients. At Kautilya we're a buy-side advisory firm, and there were literally hundreds of potential clients at this event. I didn't go in with a salesy pitch. I wanted to be genuine and talk about my thesis and my upcoming search. If someone was interested in working with Kautilya, in our help with deal sourcing or due diligence, I was more than happy to help. There have been almost a dozen conversations from this event in that direction.

*Source: 0619, 02:10-03:05.*

### Part 3: The conference and its vibe (0:45)

**On camera:** Rollup Europe was for serial acquirers, HoldCos, people who want to buy businesses, and the people who finance and invest in them. Around 250 people. They turned away a couple of hundred more, because they wanted the right fit. Of those 250, about 150 were exploring buying a business, so people like me. Amazing crowd, amazing energy.

*Source: 0618, 00:47-01:34 and 0619, 00:06-00:21.*

**On camera:** What surprised me is that the vibe wasn't very finance-coded. I've been to dozens of startup events and finance events, and they're very different. Finance is more formal and much more binary: what do you do, will that help, yes or no. Startup events are a lot less transactional: let's help each other out. This one was a mix. It had the seriousness of a finance event, with the open-mindedness and willingness to help. I really enjoy how much people are willing to help each other, and it was great to see that happen here too.

*Source: 0618, 01:34-02:35. Suggested visual: photos from the event, which Dev said he would add.*

## Script, part 4: The biggest lesson (1:15)

*Source: 0620, 00:25-01:24. Suggested visual: three short text cards, "No finance background", "No MBA", "Started late".*

*Source: 0620, 01:13-02:12.*

**On camera:** Then, with deal sourcing, I have my team, who can help with negotiations and structuring the deal. Having my team and the experience we've built up makes a huge difference.

## Script, part 6: Europe vs India (1:30)

**On camera:** So how is India different from what I saw at Rollup Europe? Very different. Three fundamental differences.

**On camera:** One, business information. In the UK you have Companies House, where you can search any business by name and see its details. India has nothing like that. We have the GST portal, but it's nowhere near the level the UK and the rest of Europe operate at.

**On camera:** Two, the investors. In Europe I saw a real desire to learn, to understand what the operator is doing and what makes their thesis different. Indian investors are very risk-averse. They don't want to truly understand what an operator is doing, they just want to know what can go wrong and how to avoid it. And there's a sheep mentality: they follow the herd, and go where things are hottest. That's not to say it never happens in Europe, but there was just so much more diversity in investor thought and approach there.

**On camera:** Three, institutional buyout firms. In Europe they're well developed, hundreds if not thousands of firms. In India there's maybe one or two dozen. Two dozen versus thousands. Something doesn't add up there.

*Source: 0622, 00:00-01:57. Suggested visual: "Europe: thousands. India: ~24" as a text card.*

**On camera:** India has a long way to go. The ecosystem is very young. There are very few deals in the lower middle market, below 100 million dollars, below 50 million, which is below 500 crores. There's jack shit happening there, guys, and it's disappointing to see. But hopefully we can help change that. We're planning one of the first acquisitions in India in the 5 to 10 crore range, around a million dollars, maybe higher. That's what I'm aiming for as my first acquisition in India.

*Source: 0622, 02:00-02:43. Optional bleep on "jack shit". The transcript says "below 100 million, below 50 million"; check the dollar and crore figures against the audio before publishing.*

**On camera:** Both have their pros and cons, but Europe is not only more evolved right now, it's simply a better market to buy in, to operate in, and to sell in. And those three things are all that matters.

*Source: 0622, 02:45-03:05.*
