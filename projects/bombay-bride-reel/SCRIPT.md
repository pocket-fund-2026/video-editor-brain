## Facts and sources (all Wikipedia; none checked against a primary source)
- Mangalsutra: the name means "an auspicious thread", knotted around the bride's neck (Wikipedia: Mangalasutra).
- Banarasi sari: a sari can take from 15 days to a month, sometimes up to six months, to complete; worn at weddings (Wikipedia: Banarasi sari).
- Paithani: first made by hand in Paithan; it flourished under the Satavahanas (2nd century BCE to 2nd century CE), who boosted its trade with Greco-Roman countries (Wikipedia: Paithani). The line says "the Roman world".
- Varmala: Sanskrit "boon garland"; the jayamala ritual is the garland exchange at a Hindu wedding (Wikipedia: Varamala).
- Mehndi: motifs can include the names or initials of the bride and groom hidden in the design (Wikipedia: Mehndi).
- Bangles: a bride commonly wears glass bangles; "according to tradition, a woman is not supposed to buy the bangles she will wear" (Wikipedia: Bangle). The line says "in some traditions".
- Lehenga: became popular in the 16th century, with variations including ghagri, A-line, flared and mermaid (Wikipedia: Lehenga).
- The markets named are the ones bombay-bride.com recommends for each item (Zaveri for gold, Kalbadevi and Mulji Jetha for cloth, Dadar for Maharashtrian bridal wear and flowers, Colaba for mehendi and bangles, Bandra for designer lehengas).
